Sheet 05 · Mechanism

Demand writes the curve.

Price is a function of on-chain state, not a book of resting orders. There's no operator setting a reference price, and no off-chain matching step between your signature and your fill.

P(s) = P₀ + ΔP · s²

Every quote — on the swap screen and inside a token's own trade panel — is read from this same function. It's the exact calculation your transaction will execute, not a separate estimate layered on top.

Sheet 06 · Protocol surface

Built for launches that become markets.

The curve is the core, not the whole product. Trading controls, distribution limits, referral accounting, and an assistant that knows the protocol ship alongside it.

01

Quotes from the contract

The swap screen and every token's trade panel read the live on-chain quote — the same math that executes your trade.

  • 0.5% · 1% · 3% presets
  • Minimum-received enforced on-chain
  • A reverted trade costs nothing
02

No wallet corners a launch

Every token caps a wallet at 5% of supply, checked on every transfer at the contract level — not just in the interface.

  • Curve and pool addresses exempt
  • Enforced on transfer
  • Raises the cost of sniping
03

Referrals paid from fee volume

Register a code once, share it, and earn a slice of protocol fees from wallets that trade under it. Share grows in tiers with volume.

  • One code per referrer
  • Permanent once set
  • Withdraw any time
04

Open or gated launches

Governance sets the mode. In gated mode, an admin clears a wallet to deploy — they never deploy on your behalf, and you stay the on-chain creator.

  • Permission gate, not a content edit
  • Metadata is never rewritten
  • Deployment fee shown up front
05

Built to be read by machines

Resolve addresses from the deployment registry, read decimals off the token, simulate before you submit, and index canonical events.

  • Never assume 18 decimals
  • Simulate to surface reverts early
  • An event for every state change
06

An assistant that knows the protocol

Ask protocol-specific questions and choose your depth — from a one-line answer to a full walk-through of the mechanism.

  • Adjustable answer depth
  • Scoped to this protocol only
  • Linked from the main nav
Sheet 07 · Boundary

Know what code guarantees. Know what governance controls.

Non-custodial execution isn't the same as governance-free. The line between the two should be visible before anyone signs anything.

Enforced on-chain

Contract guarantee

Trade pricing & execution

Priced by the curve contract and settled atomically — no off-chain matching step that could substitute a worse fill.

Slippage protection

Your signed minimum-received value is checked on-chain. A worse fill reverts instead of executing.

Max wallet limit

The 5% cap is checked on every transfer at the token contract level, not only in the interface.

Pre-graduation pool protection

The destination pool exists before the token address is public and stays locked until the curve completes.

Governance authority

Administrative scope

Launch access can be paused

Governance can pause new deployments or gate them behind approval. Live tokens aren't affected.

Wallets can be blocked from trading

The access-control layer can restrict specific addresses — a platform tool, not a creator control.

Fee parameters for new launches

Governance sets buy/sell fee caps for newly created tokens, within hard limits fixed in the contract code.

Sheet 08 · Before you sign

Questions that protect capital.

Quotes move, limits are contractual, and graduation changes the venue you're trading on. Worth knowing before your first transaction.

01

Why didn't my quote exactly match my fill?

+

The quote reflects curve state at the moment you asked for it. Any trade that lands before yours moves the price slightly — that's what your slippage tolerance is there to bound.

02

Why can't I buy more of a token?

+

You've likely hit the per-transaction cap or the max-wallet limit. Split large buys across multiple transactions, spaced so the curve can move between them.

03

Why is trading disabled on a token I created?

+

Check whether it's graduated to its own pool — trading continues there. If it hasn't, and still won't trade, the access-control layer may be paused platform-wide.

04

What happens to unsold curve tokens at graduation?

+

Remaining supply and collected ETH are paired and deposited as opening liquidity in the new pool. Nothing is returned to the creator or burned.

05

Does staking pay a fixed rate?

+

No. Displayed APR is a snapshot that moves with emissions, LP price, and participation. Treat it as an estimate, not a guarantee.
